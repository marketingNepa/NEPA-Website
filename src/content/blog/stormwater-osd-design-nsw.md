Stormwater is often where a hydraulic design quietly saves money — or quietly adds cost. Councils across NSW require most new developments to control the rate at which stormwater leaves the site, and the way that control is engineered has a real impact on buildable floor area, excavation and construction cost.

## The problem on-site detention solves

Developing a site usually increases the hard, impervious surfaces that shed water, so a storm produces more runoff, faster. If that extra flow is pushed straight into the public drainage system it can overwhelm it downstream. On-site detention (OSD) solves this by temporarily holding stormwater on the site and releasing it slowly, so the peak discharge after development is no greater than the council's **permissible site discharge** allows.

## What drives the storage volume

Two numbers dominate an OSD design:

- **Permissible site discharge (PSD)** — the maximum rate, set by council, at which water may leave the site.
- **Site storage requirement (SSR)** — the volume that must be detained to keep discharge at or below the PSD during the design storm.

Lower the allowable discharge and the required storage grows; the two are directly linked. Modelling the catchment properly — rather than applying a blanket rule — often reveals storage that is both compliant and meaningfully smaller.

## Make the tank earn its space

The best stormwater designs look for tanks that do more than one job. A detention tank can sometimes be combined with rainwater reuse storage, or shaped and positioned so it slots into otherwise unusable basement or landscape space. Thinking about this early — while the structure and civil works are still fluid — is what turns a tank from a floor-area penalty into an efficient piece of infrastructure.
