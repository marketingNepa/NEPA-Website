Rainwater reuse is one of those systems that gets specified to tick a sustainability box, then value-engineered out when the budget tightens. That's usually because it was sized and positioned as an add-on rather than designed into the hydraulic strategy from the start.

Done well, rainwater reuse does two jobs at once: it reduces mains water consumption **and** it can reduce the size — sometimes the need — of other infrastructure.

## Start with the demand, not the roof

The instinct is to size a tank from the catchment area. The better starting point is the **reuse demand**: toilet flushing, irrigation, cooling-tower make-up, wash-down. Match the tank to a demand you can actually consume year-round, and every litre collected displaces a litre you would otherwise have paid for.

An oversized tank that spends half the year full and overflowing is capital spent for no return. A right-sized tank, paired with a realistic reuse profile, pays back.

## Where reuse removes infrastructure

The bigger wins come when reuse is coordinated with stormwater:

- A reuse tank that draws down regularly provides **detention storage** between rain events, which can reduce the separate on-site detention (OSD) tank a council would otherwise require.
- Lower peak discharge can simplify downstream drainage.
- On constrained sites, combining functions into one well-designed tank frees up basement space that has real commercial value.

## The payback picture

When you add up avoided mains water, a smaller OSD requirement and reclaimed floor space, the business case shifts from "nice to have" to "pays for itself." The key is that the analysis happens early — while the hydraulic layout, the structure and the planning submission can all still move together.
